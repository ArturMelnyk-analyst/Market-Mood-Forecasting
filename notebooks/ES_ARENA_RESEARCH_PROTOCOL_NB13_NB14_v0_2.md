## 3. Notebook 13 — hypothesis and estimand

**Research question:** Does following the *original, frozen, recorded* ES classification (long/short/flat) over a feasible **post-submission** time window yield positive net per-contract returns, and how does it compare with equally timed simple trading rules?

**Primary descriptive estimand:** Mean net return per eligible scheduled challenge day, relative to that day's **entry-time ES contract notional**, including zero return on neutral/no-trade days. Also report mean net ES points per scheduled day, and per actual trade. For one ES contract, multiplier **$50 per index point**, minimum price increment **0.25 index point**, tick value **$12.50**. Contract conventions must be verified against the relevant CME spec/contract in each evaluated period.

**Do not call this account ROI.** With no stated initial margin/cash equity, leverage policy, variation margin mechanics, margin calls, funding cost, and liquidation policy, account ROI and portfolio solvency cannot be calculated credibly. An optional illustrative index based on compounded notional-normalized daily returns is an **index of synthetic normalized performance**, not a funded account equity curve.

### 3.1 Fixed trade decision and time eligibility

1. Read the saved **original** three-class probability vector and accepted direction from the same frozen challenge. Never recompute past probabilities or select a revision with knowledge of its outcome.
2. Require matching `asset=ES`, correct exact futures contract symbol, an eligible challenge and an accepted native receipt. If a legacy first-day submission lacks full receipt evidence, retain it in **historical/sensitivity analysis only**, with an explicit provenance flag; do not silently upgrade its status.
3. On a **normal full-hours** trading date, target entry **10:05:00 a.m. New York time** and exit **4:55:00 p.m. New York time**, interpreted by an IANA timezone database (`America/New_York`) rather than hard-coded UTC offsets. The required record must establish that the final forecast/accepted receipt was available **strictly before entry**; if not, log `late_signal_no_trade` and do not shift entry to a favorable time.
4. Use the **first valid contract-matched one-minute bar OPEN** stamped exactly at each predeclared time as a **last-trade execution proxy**, but only after independently validating source bar timestamp semantics. This is a simulation approximation, not a guaranteed fill. If robust bid/ask or actual executable quotations are subsequently available, run them as a separately versioned robustness analysis rather than substituting them opportunistically for some dates.
5. If the scheduled minute is missing, **no trade** (`missing_market_data`) rather than cherry-picking a later price. If exchange holiday/early close, market halt, contract rollover mismatch, anomalous bar, or no valid round-trip window: flag the day (`not_executable_under_v0_1`), report the coverage loss and distinguish it from a model error. A 10:05 entry and 16:55 exit must not be assumed on special-hours sessions.
6. One eligible signal creates at most one round-trip. Close at the scheduled exit irrespective of interim high/low, Arena result, or the direction the market ultimately settles. No intraday adjustment, trailing stop, take-profit, dynamic order switching, or overnight hold in v0.1.
7. Market data may be acquired *after the fact* solely for scoring the immutable, previously committed trade rule; market observations after each signal may **never** change signal selection, time, direction or price-field choice.

**Important execution nuance:** A one-minute OHLCV open is not a historical fill receipt or a contemporaneously accessible post-quote confirmation. Label the whole study *bar-open-proxy paper execution* unless timestamped quotes/trades can establish a stronger fill model. Use only the same convention for all dates.

### 3.2 Trade arithmetic and cost scenarios

Let `d ∈ {+1, 0, −1}` denote long, flat or short, `P_in`, `P_out` the positive ES proxy prices, `M=50 USD/point`, `tick=0.25 point`, `k` the number of adverse ticks **per side**, and `f` the all-in explicit cash fee **USD per side, per contract**. For each traded day:

- Gross points = `d × (P_out − P_in)`.
- Gross USD P&L = `M × gross_points`.
- Net USD P&L = `M × [gross_points − 2 × k × tick] − 2 × f`.
- Net notional-normalized daily return = `net USD P&L / (M × P_in)`.
- Neutral (`d=0`) = zero trade, zero fee, zero return; distinguish from data-missing days, which must not be implicitly scored as successful cash decisions.

For short positions, `d = −1` reverses the sign of the *market price difference*; costs remain negative. The formula assumes symmetric adverse price adjustment and a common contract on both sides. Costs are charged only when a trade occurs.

| Scenario | Adverse proxy fill adjustment | Explicit assumed cash fee | Round-trip cost per 1 ES, excluding market move | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idealized | 0 ticks / side | $0 / side | $0 | Gross upper reference, **not realistic execution** |
| **Base (primary net)** | **1 tick / side** | **$2 / side** | **$29** | *Illustrative assumption*, not verified broker execution |
| Stress | 2 ticks / side | $4 / side | $58 | Conservative sensitivity, not a claim about historical spreads |

The price adjustment is **one combined spread/slippage allowance**: do not additionally subtract a full spread on top unless documented as a separate non-overlapping cost component. The fee amounts above are stipulated scenario values, **not verified exchange/broker invoices**. If exact broker+exchange fees are later verified, add them as a separately labeled scenario rather than covertly replacing the predeclared base case. Interest/taxes/FX/funding/margin are not modeled in this notional-normalized study; clearly disclose consequences for real-world trading.

### 3.3 Benchmarks and reporting

- **Primary benchmark:** Flat/no trade (zero P&L, no costs). It establishes whether modeled trades net positive, not whether forecasting outperforms a sensible active alternative.
- **Matched-time always-long ES:** Same dates, exact contract, entry, exit, fill proxy and costs. Compare only common execution-valid dates, report denominators.
- **Optional matched-time always-short ES:** Same treatment; label secondary.
- **Optional chance-direction rule:** If used, pre-specify deterministic random seed and baseline probabilities from *training only*, not evaluation labels. Do not cherry-pick the better random draw.

Report both **per eligible scheduled day** and **per trade** where meaningful: eligible days, accepted forecasts, executed trades, skipped due to neutral, excluded due to data-quality or late-signal, long/short counts, gross/net points, gross/net one-contract USD P&L, normalized daily mean and aggregate, total fees/friction, win rate on trades, and an **artificial** unit-notional cumulative index (base 100) with index drawdown, carefully identified as not an account balance. Give side-by-side baseline cost scenarios and no-cost sensitivity. Plot uncertainty/dispersion but avoid reliable annualized Sharpe/ROI claims on a very small sample.

**Comparator fairness:** Always-long/short must be evaluated on exactly the same valid market windows. For a model-neutral day, the model is flat while always-long still trades; count that as an intentional strategy difference. Report separately whether missing-price days prevent matched comparison. No use of hindsight to select a direction, market day, or price.

## 4. Notebook 14 — volatility and geopolitical sensitivity design

**Research question:** Across the fixed ES prediction cohort, is error magnitude, classification quality or hypothetical economic value descriptively different in periods of higher *pre-forecast* volatility or documented market-moving geopolitical events? We will describe associations, **not causal effects**.

### 4.1 Pre-forecast volatility regimes

Use **Cboe VIX Index**, explicitly **not** VIX futures or a generic equity-volatility surrogate, with the **latest fully published VIX daily closing level prior to the ES forecast submission**. When a prior-day close is unavailable, leave regime as missing; do not use the same day's subsequent VIX close or backfill from later observations.

Pre-specify these simple **illustrative, fixed absolute buckets**, not optimized using the 30 outcomes:

- `low`: prior-available VIX `<15`;
- `medium`: `15 ≤ VIX < 25`;
- `high`: VIX `≥25`;
- `missing`: no validated pre-forecast value.

The 15/25 cutoffs are an a priori design convention, **not a claim of universal statistical regime boundaries**. Their appropriateness and source coverage must be described in the notebook. A separately labeled robustness analysis may use ES realized volatility computed **only from prior completed ES sessions**, e.g. 20-session standard deviation of completed-session returns (no future or contemporaneous settlement leakage). Do not use evaluation-period quantiles to fit bins and then label them preregistered.

### 4.2 Event chronology and information cutoff

Events must be **logged chronologically with source and time**, independently of whether the model was right or wrong:

| Field | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| `event_id`, `event_type` | Stable ID; predeclared category: armed-conflict escalation / sanctions-trade / monetary-policy / scheduled macro release / other documented macro shock |
| `event_occurred_at_utc` | Best supported event occurrence time; if only the calendar date is known, record time as unknown |
| `first_publication_at_utc` | Earliest independently sourced publication time available; cite dated primary institution / authoritative reporting |
| `source_url`, `accessed_at_utc` | Where the fact and timestamps were obtained |
| `forecast_available_at_utc` | Actual forecast/receipt information cutoff |
| `known_before_forecast` | `yes`, `no` or `unknown`, based on timestamp evidence, never inference from outcome |
| `relative_event_window` | `before_forecast`, `after_forecast_before_exit`, `after_exit`, or `undetermined` |
| `coding_notes` | Objective evidence and any temporal ambiguity; separate factual event from interpretive market narrative |

Do not claim the agent could have used information first published later. For uncertainty in release time, mark `unknown`, never manufacture time-of-day. Include non-geopolitical economic events (Fed decisions, CPI, employment releases) to expose confounding; do not assign effects to political actors based on anecdotal outcomes.

**Registration before analysis:** Write event-category definitions and a reproducible source-selection rule before viewing subgroup performance. Historical event extraction performed now is **retrospective exploratory annotation**, even if the event was known before a particular 2026 forecast. Do not select event windows solely because a model prediction failed. Use a dated chronology with all events meeting the rule within the declared calendar range, including days with correct forecasts.

### 4.3 Metrics, uncertainty and noncausal interpretation

Notebook 14 should join the original accepted-and-settled Notebook 12 challenge ledger while **retaining separate provenance and timing flags** (and a read-only Notebook 13 trade ledger only if the paper-execution protocol qualifies that subset). Strict-time-certified-only results, where supported, are a separate sensitivity analysis; do not silently discard every Arena-accepted forecast because its server timezone is unresolved. Compute by regime/event group: count and coverage, class composition, accuracy, **unscaled 3-class Brier** (consistent with Notebook 12), log loss when defined, mean maximum confidence, forecast uncertainty, and optional net normalized trade outcome if the execution sample is available. Always show overall comparators, available missingness, and uncertainty intervals with small-sample and temporal dependence caveats.

**Statistical restrictions:** Thirty ES challenges are **not** thirty independent macro regimes or a robust basis for event-specific causal inference. Avoid numerous significance tests, post hoc searching for cutoffs, and confident claims from sparse cells. Emphasize descriptive effect size and coverage, show individual-date points if useful, suppress or mark unstable subgroup summaries for groups with <5 observations, do not call an empty group “zero risk,” and do not use later events to classify past information sets. Negative findings and null differences belong in the final report.
